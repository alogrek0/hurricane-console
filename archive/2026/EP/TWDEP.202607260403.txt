Tropical Weather Discussion for the eastern Pacific Ocean from
03.4S to 30N, east of 120W including the Gulf of California, and
from the Equator to 30N, between 120W and 140W. The following
information is based on satellite imagery, weather observations,
radar, and meteorological analysis.

Based on 0000 UTC surface analysis and satellite imagery through
0345 UTC.
 
...SPECIAL FEATURES...

Hurricane Fausto is centered near 19.5N 138.8W, or about 920 nm 
east of Hilo, Hawaii at 26/0300 UTC, moving west at 11 kt. 
Estimated minimum central pressure is 971 mb. Maximum sustained 
wind speed is 85 kt with gusts to 105 kt. Peak seas are to around 
34 ft. Satellite imagery indicates that Fausto's overall 
structure has changed little during the past several hours. The 
imagery reveals numerous moderate to strong convection within 
120 nm of the center, except 90 nm in the NE quadrant. Fausto is
forecast move west of 140W on Sun morning, however, fresh to 
near gale-force winds will linger from 18N to 25N west of 136W 
along with seas of 8 to 11 ft. These conditions will subside by 
early Mon afternoon as Fausto continues to well west of the 
discussion area. 

Recently upgraded Hurricane Genevieve is centered near 12.1N
106.7W, or roughly about 430 nm south-southwest of Manzanillo, 
Mexico at 8 PM PDT, and is moving west-northwest at 13 kt. 
Estimated minimum central pressure is 987 mb. Peak seas are to 27
ft. Satellite imagery that Genevieve is quickly becoming bettor
organized and intensifying. Well-established upper-level outflow 
is noted in nearly all of the quadrants. The imagery reveals 
banding features wrapping around its center. Numerous strong 
convection is within 90 nm of the center in the NE quadrant, 
within 120 nm of the center in the SE quadrant and 60 nm in the 
SW quadrant. Numerous moderate to isolated strong convection 
denotes an outer band within 90 nm of a line from 10N105W to 
07N108W, and within 30 nm of a line from 07N108W to 5.5N112W and 
to 06N117W. A west-northwestward to northwestward motion with 
some decrease in forward speed is expected during the next few 
days. Rapid intensification is expected during the next day or 
so, and Genevieve is forecast to become a major hurricane by late
Sun. Swells generated by Genevieve are affecting portions of the
southwestern coast of Mexico and will likely reach southern 
portions of the Baja California Peninsula Sun night or on Mon. 
These swells are likely to cause life-threatening surf and rip 
current conditions. Please consult products from your local 
weather office. Genevieve is forecast to become a hurricane by 
tonight and a major hurricane by Monday.

...TROPICAL WAVES...

A tropical wave is along 90W north of 03N to inland central
Guatemala. It is moving westward at 10 to 15 kt. Isolated showers 
and thunderstorms are near its southern portion, and over some 
sections of Guatemala.

...INTERTROPICAL CONVERGENCE ZONE/MONSOON TROUGH...

The monsoon trough axis extends from low pressure over northwest 
Colombia west-southwestward to just north of Panama, to across 
southern Costa Rica, to 09N84W, to 06N90W and to 08N95W. It 
resume south of Hurricane Genevieve near 09.5N107W to 08N114W,
where it transitions to the ITCZ to 08N120W and to 09N127W. 
Scattered moderate convection is within 120 nm south of the 
ITCZ between 117W-124W.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF MEXICO...

A surface ridge extends northwest to southwest over the waters 
west of Baja California. The gradient between the ridge and 
relatively lower pressures over Baja California is supporting 
moderate to fresh northwest winds over the waters west of Baja 
California, primarily north of Punta Eugenia, mostly gentle
northwest to north winds south of Punta Eugenia. Seas over 
the waters west of Baja California are 4 to 6 ft in northwest 
swell, except for slightly higher seas of 5 to 7 ft north of 
Punta Eugenia. Light and variable winds are over waters southeast
of Baja California to near 18N along with seas of 3 to 5 ft in 
south to southwest swell. Fresh to strong east to southeast winds
in the northern outer periphery of now Hurricane Genevieve are 
present south of 18N along with seas 7 to 11 ft in southeast to 
south swell.

For the forecast, Genevieve will rapidly intensify as it moves 
to near 13.0N 108.5W Sun morning with maximum sustained winds 
gusts 80 kt gusts 100 kt, to near 14.2N 110.5W Sun evening, to 
near 15.3N 112.2W Mon morning with maximum sustained winds 110 kt
gusts 135 kt, to near 16.5N 113.7W Mon evening, to near 17.3N 
115.0W Tue morning, and to near 18.1N 116.4W Tue evening with 
maximum sustained winds 105 kt gusts 130 kt. Genevieve is 
forecast to change little in intensity as it moves to near 19.3N 
119.7W Wed evening. As Genevieve tracks in a general west- 
northwestward to northwestward motion west of Baja California Sur
next week, the pressure gradient between it and high pressure to
its north the pressure gradient will tighten west of Baja 
California Norte. Swells from Genevieve will impact just about 
all of the offshore waters outside the Gulf of California, except
for its entrance through next week building seas over these 
waters to 12 ft and greater.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF CENTRAL AMERICA, COLOMBIA, 
AND WITHIN 750 NM OF ECUADOR...

Fresh northeast winds continue across the Papagayo region and 
extending downwind to near 90W, and northward to southern 
Nicaragua. Seas are 5 to 7 ft with these winds. Scattered showers 
are over most of the offshore waters north of Costa Rica. 
Elsewhere, winds are moderate or weaker with seas 4 to 5 ft in S 
to SW swell.

For the forecast, the fresh to strong northeast to east winds
in the Gulf of Papagayo region will pulse across the Papagayo 
region well into next week. Moderate to rough seas will accompany
these winds. Gentle to moderate north winds are expected in the 
Gulf of Panama into early next week with slight to moderate seas.
Moderate or weaker winds and moderate seas in southwest swell 
are expected elsewhere, except in the immediate lee of the 
Galapagos Islands and nearshore western Colombia where slight 
seas are forecast. The southwest swell will subside around 
midweek.

Tropical Weather Discussion for the eastern Pacific Ocean from
03.4S to 30N, east of 120W including the Gulf of California, and
from the Equator to 30N, between 120W and 140W. The following
information is based on satellite imagery, weather observations,
radar, and meteorological analysis.

Based on 1800 UTC surface analysis and satellite imagery through
1945 UTC.
 
...SPECIAL FEATURES...

Hurricane Genevieve is centered near 20.8N 124.8W at 30/2100
UTC, moving west-northwest at 13 kt. Estimated minimum central
pressure is 984 mb. Maximum sustained wind speed is 65 kt with
gusts to 80 kt. Peak seas are near 31 ft or 9.5 m. Numerous 
moderate to strong convection is within 30 nm in the SE
semicircle, and 60 nm in the NW semicircle. Scattered moderate
convection is noted elsewhere within 180 nm of the center of
Genevieve. Genevieve is moving toward the west-northwest, and this
general motion is expected to continue during the next several 
days. Gradual weakening is forecast during the next few days, 
and Genevieve is expected to weaken to a tropical storm later 
today and become a remnant low late this weekend. 

Swells generated by Genevieve are affecting portions of the
Baja California Peninsula and Southern California. These swells will
persist for the next several days, and are likely to cause
life-threatening surf and rip current conditions. Please consult
products from your local weather office.
 
A depiction of rip current risk for the United States can be found

...TROPICAL WAVES...

A tropical wave is moving through the western Caribbean Sea with
the southern extent now in the far eastern Pacific waters along
79W to the north of 02N through the Gulf of Panama, moving
quickly westward around 25 kt. Numerous moderate scattered strong
convection is noted from 01N to 10N between 77W and 87W.

A tropical wave is along 100W, extending from 01N to 16N near 
the coast of Guerrero, Mexico, moving westward around 20 kt. 
Scattered moderate isolated strong convection is noted from 05N
to 07.5N between 98W and 105W.

A tropical wave is along 110.5W, from 03N to 19N near the 
Revillagigedo Islands, moving westward around 5 to 10 kt. 
Isolated to widely scattered moderate to strong convection is 
noted from 03N to 23N between 105W and 116W.

...INTERTROPICAL CONVERGENCE ZONE/MONSOON TROUGH...

The monsoon trough extends from 10N84W to 07N90W. The ITCZ
extends from 07N90W to 06N104W to 12N121W with breaks near the
above tropical waves. A monsoon trough segment extends from
southwest of Genevieve at 14N127W to 07N140W. Other than the
convection mentioned above, scattered moderate is noted from 08N
to 11.5N between 121W and 127W, and from 03N to 09N between 132W
and 140W.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF MEXICO...

Please refer to the Special Features Section above for details on
Hurricane Genevieve which continues to move away from the outer
offshore waters of Baja California. Please refer to the Tropical
Waves section above for information about additional convection 
in the region.

Swells from Hurricane Genevieve are still bringing some rough 
seas to around 8 ft to the outer offshore waters of Baja 
California Sur. Scattered convection, likely inducing locally 
gusty winds and locally higher seas is occurring from near the
entrance to the Gulf of California to near and south of Cabo San
Lucas, also likely enhanced by the northern portion of the 
tropical wave along 110.5W. Moderate to locally fresh N gap 
winds are in the Gulf of Tehuantepec this afternoon. Elsewhere, 
moderate or weaker winds prevail. Seas are slight in the Gulf of 
California and moderate in the remaining offshore waters.

For the forecast, rough seas in the far outer offshore waters
west of Baja California will subside this evening. Increasing NW
swell may lead to rough seas again offshore Baja California 
Norte Sun night and Mon. Fresh to locally strong gap winds are 
likely in the Gulf of Tehuantepec tonight, then moderate to fresh
Fri night. Moderate to fresh NW winds are expected to pulse 
tonight and Fri night nearshore Baja California as high pressure 
builds southward west of the Peninsula. Elsewhere, Fresh SE to S 
winds may pulse nightly in the northern Gulf of California 
beginning tonight, possibly to strong Sat night.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF CENTRAL AMERICA, COLOMBIA, 
AND WITHIN 750 NM OF ECUADOR...

Please refer to the Tropical Waves section above for information
about convection in the region. 

High pressure northeast of the area is forcing fresh to strong 
northeasterly gap winds and resultant seas of 7 to 9 ft across 
the Gulf of Papagayo region. Elsewhere, light to gentle winds 
and moderate seas prevail. 

For the forecast, fresh to strong NE to E gap winds will pulse 
across the Papagayo region through Sun, with occasionally rough 
seas. Moderate to fresh winds will then pulse thereafter. Pulsing
moderate to locally fresh N winds are expected in the Gulf of 
Panama tonight. Southerly winds may freshen offshore southern 
Colombia and from Ecuador to the Galapagos Islands tonight and 
Fri. Moderate or weaker winds and moderate seas in SW swell are 
expected across the remainder of the waters through the period. 

...REMAINDER OF THE AREA...

Please refer to the Special Features section for information on
Hurricane Genevieve.

For waters not being impacted by Hurricane Genevieve, high
pressure is centered just northwest of the area. The pressure 
pattern between the high and Genevieve is supporting moderate to 
fresh NE trades to the north and west of Genevieve, mainly across
the waters north of 17N and west of 125W. Seas in this region 
have built to 7 to 10 ft well away from Genevieve including
elsewhere from 12N to 30N to the west of 117W. Moderate to 
locally fresh SE winds are found south of the Equator and west of
100W. Moderate or weaker winds are elsewhere. Moderate seas 
dominate the remainder of the waters.

For the forecast, Hurricane Genevieve will weaken to a tropical
storm near 21.2N 126.7W Fri morning, move to 21.9N 129.3W Fri 
afternoon, 22.7N 132.1W Sat morning, 23.6N 135.1W Sat afternoon, 
24.4N 138.0W Sun morning, and become post-tropical and move just
west of the discussion waters to 25.4N 140.5W Sun afternoon. 
Genevieve will weaken to a remnant low near 27.7N 144.5W 
Mon afternoon. Away from Genevieve, little change in marine 
conditions is forecast through the weekend. Fresh and rough 
northerly swell off California is likely to push southward into 
the waters off Baja California Norte north of 27N and east of 
130W by the end of the weekend into early next week. Looking 
ahead, new large southerly swell may build south of the Equator 
and west of 100W beginning Tue night.

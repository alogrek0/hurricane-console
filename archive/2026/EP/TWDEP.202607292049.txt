Tropical Weather Discussion for the eastern Pacific Ocean from
03.4S to 30N, east of 120W including the Gulf of California, and
from the Equator to 30N, between 120W and 140W. The following
information is based on satellite imagery, weather observations,
radar, and meteorological analysis.

Based on 1800 UTC surface analysis and satellite imagery through
1930 UTC.
 
...SPECIAL FEATURES...

Hurricane Genevieve is centered near 19.2N 120.1W at 29/2100
UTC, moving west-northwest at 8 kt. Estimated minimum central
pressure is 970 mb. Maximum sustained wind speed is 85 kt with
gusts to 105 kt. Peak seas are near 36 ft or 11 m. Numerous 
moderate isolated strong convection is within 180 nm in the NE 
quadrant, 300 nm in the SE quadrant, and 150 nm in the W 
semicircle. Genevieve is moving toward the west-northwest. This 
general motion with a slight increase in forward speed is 
expected during the next several days. Steady weakening is 
expected during the next couple of days, and Genevieve is 
forecast to become post-tropical this weekend. Swells generated
by Genevieve are affecting portions of the Baja California 
Peninsula and will reach Southern California today. These swells 
will persist for the next several days and are likely to cause 
life-threatening surf and rip current conditions. Please consult 
products from your local weather office. Please read the latest 
HIGH SEAS FORECAST issued by the National Hurricane Center at 
website - https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/text/MIAHSFEP2.shtml and the 
latest Genevieve NHC Forecast/Advisory and Public Advisory at 
www.hurricanes.gov for more details.

A tropical wave is along 107.5W, from 03N to 19N, moving very
slowly W at around 5 kt. Any nearby convection is described 
below with the ITCZ/monsoon trough.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF MEXICO...

Please refer to the Special Features section for information on
Hurricane Genevieve with tropical storm force or greater winds 
just beyond the Baja California Sur offshore waters. Please 
refer to the Tropical Waves and Monsoon Trough/ITCZ sections 
above for information about additional convection in the region.

Away from the impacts of Genevieve, moderate or weaker winds 
prevail. Seas are slight in the Gulf of California and moderate 
in the remaining offshore waters, except rough to very rough west
of 112W and the outer offshore waters of Baja California due to 
Genevieve.

For the forecast, Hurricane Genevieve will move to 19.7N 121.8W 
Thu morning, 20.4N 124.2W Thu afternoon, weaken to a tropical 
storm near 21.2N 126.6W Fri morning, 22.0N 129.2W Fri afternoon, 
22.7N 132.0W Sat morning, and 23.5N 134.9W Sat afternoon. 
Genevieve will become post-tropical as it moves to 25.4N 140.2W 
Sun afternoon. Associated seas at rough or greater heights will 
finally subside across the offshore waters of Baja California 
through the day Thu. Elsewhere, fresh northerly winds are likely 
in the Gulf of Tehunatepec Thu and Fri nights. Otherwise, areas 
of fresh mainly westerly gap winds are expected through Thu night
nearshore Baja California as high pressure builds southward to 
the west of the Baja California Peninsula. Moderate to locally 
fresh SE to S winds may pulse in the northern Gulf of California 
beginning this weekend. Looking ahead, rough northerly seas may 
briefly build south of 30N into the waters off Baja California 
Norte by the end of the weekend into early next week.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF CENTRAL AMERICA, COLOMBIA, 
AND WITHIN 750 NM OF ECUADOR...

Please refer to the Monsoon Trough/ITCZ and Tropical Wave
sections above for information about convection in the region. 

High pressure northeast of the area is forcing fresh easterly 
gap winds and resultant seas of 5 to 7 ft across the Gulf of 
Papagayo region, with moderate northerly gap winds in the Gulf of
Panama. Elsewhere, light to gentle winds and moderate seas 
prevail.

For the forecast, fresh to strong NE to E gap winds will pulse 
across the Papagayo region through the weekend, with occasionally
rough seas. Pulsing moderate to locally fresh N winds are 
expected in the Gulf of Panama tonight. Southerly winds may 
freshen offshore southern Colombia and from Ecuador to the 
Galapagos Islands Thu night and Fri. Moderate or weaker winds and
moderate seas in SW swell are expected across the remainder of 
the waters through the period.

Tropical Weather Discussion for the eastern Pacific Ocean from
03.4S to 30N, east of 120W including the Gulf of California, and
from the Equator to 30N, between 120W and 140W. The following
information is based on satellite imagery, weather observations,
radar, and meteorological analysis.

Based on 1200 UTC surface analysis and satellite imagery through
1500 UTC.
 
...SPECIAL FEATURES...

Hurricane Genevieve is centered near 17.9N 116.1W at 28/1500
UTC, moving west-northwest at 8 kt. Estimated minimum central
pressure is 950 mb. Maximum sustained wind speed is 110 kt with
gusts to 135 kt. Peak seas are near 40 to 41 ft, or 12.5 m. 
Numerous moderate to strong convection is within 60 nm of the
center, except 120 nm in the SE quadrant. Scattered to numerous
moderate convection is elsewhere within 240 nm in the SE
semicircle and 120 nm in the NW semicircle. Genevieve is moving
toward the west-northwest. A west- northwestward motion is 
expected during the next few days. Genevieve is a category 3 
hurricane on the Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale. Steady 
weakening is forecast over the next several days. Swells 
generated by Genevieve are affecting portions of the southwestern
coast of Mexico and the Baja California Peninsula. These swells 
are likely to persist for a few more days, and could cause life-
threatening surf and rip current conditions. Please consult 
products from your local weather office. Please read the latest 
HIGH SEAS FORECAST issued by the National Hurricane Center at 
website - https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/text/MIAHSFEP2.shtml and the 
latest Genevieve NHC Forecast/Advisory and Public Advisory at 
www.hurricanes.gov for more details.

...TROPICAL WAVES...

A tropical wave is along 82W, from 05N northward into the
Caribbean Sea, moving westward at around 5 kt. Any nearby
convection is described in the ITCZ/monsoon trough section below.

A tropical wave is along 104W, from 04N to 15N, moving westward 
at around 20 kt. Scattered moderate isolated strong convection 
is observed from 02N to 09.5N between 95W and 106W. Isolated to
scattered moderate convection is noted from 15N to 19N between
102W and 109W.

...INTERTROPICAL CONVERGENCE ZONE/MONSOON TROUGH...

The monsoon trough extends from 10.5N74W to 08N94W to 12N106W,
then resumes from 11N121W to 07N140W. Scattered moderate isolated
strong convection is noted from 07N to 09.5N between 83W and 88W,
from 02N to 05N between 85W and 93W, and within 240 nm either
side of the monsoon trough between 125W and 140W.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF MEXICO...

Please refer to the Special Features section for information on
Major Hurricane Genevieve. Please refer to the Tropical Waves 
and Monsoon Trough/ITCZ sections above for information about 
additional convection in the region.

Away from the impacts of Genevieve, moderate to locally fresh NW
winds associated with high pressure building in from the 
northwest are impacting waters N and W of Guadalupe Island. 
Outflow from convection over Sonora is bringing fresh SE winds to
the northern Gulf of California. Elsewhere, moderate or weaker 
winds prevail. Seas are slight in the Gulf of California and 
moderate in the remaining offshore waters.

For the forecast, Hurricane Genevieve will move to 18.3N 117.2W
this evening, 18.8N 118.9W Wed morning, 19.5N 120.7W Wed 
evening, 20.2N 122.9W Thu morning, weaken to a tropical storm 
near 20.9N 125.3W Thu evening, and 21.6N 127.7W Fri morning. 
Genevieve will change little in intensity as it moves to 23.1N 
133.0W early Sat. Large seas associated with Genevieve will 
finally pull away from the offshore waters of Baja California 
Fri. Elsewhere, areas of fresh mainly westerly gap winds are 
likely through Wed night nearshore Baja California and also in 
the Gulf of California as high pressure builds southward to the 
west of the Baja California Peninsula. Fresh northerly winds may
pulse in the Gulf of Tehuantepec at night into the early morning
hours Thu night and Fri night. Looking ahead, fresh and rough 
northerly swell off California may push southward into the waters
off Baja California Norte by the end of the upcoming weekend.

Tropical Weather Discussion for the eastern Pacific Ocean from
03.4S to 30N, east of 120W including the Gulf of California, and
from the Equator to 30N, between 120W and 140W. The following
information is based on satellite imagery, weather observations,
radar, and meteorological analysis.

Based on 1200 UTC surface analysis and satellite imagery through
1500 UTC.
 
...SPECIAL FEATURES...

Hurricane Genevieve is centered near 16.0N 113.1W at 27/1500
UTC, moving northwest at 11 kt. Estimated minimum central
pressure is 929 mb. Maximum sustained wind speed is 135 kt with
gusts to 165 kt. Peak seas are near 44 ft or 13.5 m. Numerous 
strong convection is noted within 75 nm of the center of
Genevieve. Numerous moderate convection is noted elsewhere within
210 of the center, except 180 nm in the NW quadrant, and in a
band between 240 nm and 510 nm in the S quadrant. Genevieve is 
moving toward the northwest. A west-northwestward to 
northwestward motion with some decrease in forward speed is 
expected during the next couple of days. Genevieve is a category 
4 hurricane on the Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale. Some 
fluctuations in intensity are likely today. Steady weakening is 
forecast tonight through the remainder of the week. Swells 
generated by Genevieve are affecting portions of the southwestern
coast of Mexico and southern portions of the Baja California 
Peninsula, and will spread northward along the remainder of the 
west coast of the Baja California Peninsula by tonight. These 
swells are likely to cause life-threatening surf and rip current 
conditions. Please consult products from your local weather 
office. Please read the latest HIGH SEAS FORECAST issued by the 
National Hurricane Center at website - 
https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/text/MIAHSFEP2.shtml and the latest 
Genevieve NHC Forecast/Advisory and Public Advisory at 
www.hurricanes.gov for more details.

...TROPICAL WAVES...

A tropical wave is along 97W, south of 15N just off the coast of
southern Mexico, moving westward at around 10 kt. Scattered 
moderate convection is observed from 03N to 07N between 95W and
101W, and also from 12N to 14N between 98W and 102W.

...INTERTROPICAL CONVERGENCE ZONE/MONSOON TROUGH...

The monsoon trough extends from 1011 mb low pressure over
northern Colombia from 10N75W to 11N81W to 07N97W to 12N107W,
then resumes SW of Genevieve from 10N118W to 05N138W. The ITCZ 
stretches from 05N138W to beyond 04N140W. Scattered to numerous 
moderate isolated strong convection is present from 01N to 10N
between 77W and 89W, from 06N to 08N between 117W and 123W, from
10N to 11.5N between 120W and 124W, from 00N to 05N between 125W
and 137W, and from 04.5N to 07.5N between 135.5W and 140W.
Similar convection is noted within 150 nm of the coast of Mexico
between 100W and 109.5W. 

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF MEXICO...

Please refer to the Special Features section for information on
Major Hurricane Genevieve, currently located SSW of the
Revillagigedo Islands

Away form the impacts of Genevieve, moderate to fresh N gap winds
are in the Tehunatepec region. Moderate to locally fresh NW 
winds associated with high pressure building in from the N are 
impacting waters N and W of Guadalupe Island. Elsewhere, light to
gentle winds prevail. Seas are slight in the Gulf of California 
and moderate in the remaining offshore waters. 

For the forecast, Hurricane  Genevieve will move to 16.8N 114.3W
this evening, 17.7N 115.8W Tue morning, 18.2N 117.3W Tue 
evening, 18.8N 118.9W Wed morning, 19.5N 120.7W Wed evening, and 
20.4N 122.8W Thu morning. Genevieve will weaken to a tropical 
storm over 22.1N 127.8W early Fri. Large seas associated with

California by the end of the week. Elsewhere, areas of fresh 
mainly westerly gap winds are likely tonight through Wed night in
the Gulf of California as high pressure builds southward to the 
west of the Baja California Peninsula.

Tropical Weather Discussion for the eastern Pacific Ocean from
03.4S to 30N, east of 120W including the Gulf of California, and
from the Equator to 30N, between 120W and 140W. The following
information is based on satellite imagery, weather observations,
radar, and meteorological analysis.

Based on 1200 UTC surface analysis and satellite imagery through
1505 UTC.

...TROPICAL WAVES...

The axis of a tropical wave is near 87W, north of 02N, moving 
westward at 10 to 15 kt. A few showers are noted near the trough
axis.

The axis of a tropical wave is near 109W, moving westward at 
around 10 to 15 kt. A few showers are present near the trough 
axis.

The axis of a tropical wave is near 127W, from 02N to 19N, 
moving westward around 10 kt. Scattered moderate to strong
convection is observed from 03N to 17N and between 114W and 131W.

...INTERTROPICAL CONVERGENCE ZONE/MONSOON TROUGH...

The monsoon trough extends from 07N77W to 11N110W to 13N120W to 05N140W.
Scattered moderate to isolated strong convection is occurring 
from 02N to 18N and east of 114W.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF MEXICO...

The subtropical ridge in the NE Pacific is centered off the
Pacific Northwest of the United States, allowing for a ridge to
extend southeastward to the Revillagigedo Islands. The weak ridge
supports mainly moderate or weaker winds and slight to moderate 
seas across much of the Mexican offshore waters, including the 
Gulfs of California and Tehuantepec. A subsiding northerly swell
well west of Baja California Norte still produces seas to 8 ft 
to the northwest of Guadalupe Island. A few showers and isolated
thunderstorms are noted off southern and SW Mexico. Gusty winds
and suddenly higher seas are possible near the stronger storms.

For the forecast, the pressure gradient between high pressure 
NW of the area and low pressure over the Colorado River Valley 
will support moderate to occasionally fresh winds over the 
northern Gulf of California through the early part of the week. 
Elsewhere, a moderate pressure gradient will support mostly 
gentle to moderate winds across the offshore waters of Mexico. 
Long-period NW swell over the waters off Baja California Norte 
will start to subside Tue. Fresh to strong gap winds and building
seas are expected across the Gulf of Tehuantepec toward the end 
of the week.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF CENTRAL AMERICA, COLOMBIA, 
AND WITHIN 750 NM OF ECUADOR...

High pressure north of the area continues to support moderate to
locally fresh winds and moderate seas in the Gulf of Papagayo,
extending downstream to about 90W. Gentle to moderate winds are 
found between the Galapagos Islands and Ecuador. Light to gentle 
winds and moderate seas are noted elsewhere. 

For the forecast, moderate to fresh winds will pulse across the
Papagayo region, with occasionally strong winds pulsing by mid 
week. Moderate or weaker winds and moderate seas in SW swell are
expected across the remainder of the waters through the period. 

...REMAINDER OF THE AREA...

The remnants of former Hurricane Genevieve continue to move away
from the tropical eastern Pacific. However, moderate to fresh
easterly winds and seas of 8-10 ft are still present north of 23N
and west of 137W. In the northern waters, moderate northerly 
winds and seas of 7-9 ft are found north of 28N and east of 130W.
High pressure dominates the remainder of the area, sustaining
moderate or weaker winds and moderate seas.

For the forecast, subsiding northerly swell will bring rough 
seas over the waters off Baja California Norte north of 27N and 
east of 130W through Tue. Southerly swell will build northward to
03N and west of 105W starting Tue night. Meanwhile, seas may build
to 8 ft in the western trade waters late this week.

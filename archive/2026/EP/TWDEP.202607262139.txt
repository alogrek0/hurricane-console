Tropical Weather Discussion for the eastern Pacific Ocean from
03.4S to 30N, east of 120W including the Gulf of California, and
from the Equator to 30N, between 120W and 140W. The following
information is based on satellite imagery, weather observations,
radar, and meteorological analysis.

Based on 1800 UTC surface analysis and satellite imagery through
2135 UTC.
 
...SPECIAL FEATURES...

Hurricane Genevieve is centered near 13.9N 110.3W at 26/2100 
UTC, moving west-northwest at 12 kt. Estimated minimum central 
pressure is 947 mb. Maximum sustained wind speed is 120 kt with 
gusts to 145 kt. Peak seas are near 35 ft (10.5 M). A west- 
northwestward to northwestward motion with some decrease in 
forward speed is expected during the next few days. Genevieve is
a category 4 hurricane on the Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale.
Continued strengthening is expected during the next day or so. 
Genevieve is then forecast to start weakening by the middle of 
this week. Swells generated by Genevieve are affecting portions 
of the southwestern coast of Mexico and will likely reach 
southern portions of the Baja California Peninsula tonight or 
Monday. These swells are likely to cause life-threatening surf 
and rip current conditions. Please consult products from your 
local weather office.

Please read the latest HIGH SEAS FORECAST issued by the National
Hurricane Center at website - 
https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/text/MIAHSFEP2.shtml and the latest NHC 
Forecast/Advisories and Public Advisories of both systems at 
www.hurricanes.gov for more details.

observed between 90W and 100W.

...INTERTROPICAL CONVERGENCE ZONE/MONSOON TROUGH...

The monsoon trough extends from 10N85W to 07N93W to 09N99W and

07N128W. A surface trough is along 130W, south of 12N. Scattered

between 125W and 140W.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF MEXICO...

Please refer to the Special Features section for details on 
Hurricane Genevieve, currently located a few hundred miles SW of
Mexico.

The pressure gradient between high pressure over the southern 
United States and lower pressures in the tropics results in fresh
N-NE winds across the Gulf of Tehuantepec. Seas in these waters 
are 6-8 ft. A similar gradient is bringing moderate to fresh NW
winds and seas of 5-7 ft to the waters west of Guadalupe Island.
In the remainder of the offshore waters of Baja California, light
to gentle winds and moderate seas are present. Moderate to
locally fresh S-SE winds and seas of 4-6 ft are noted in the
northern Gulf of California waters, while lighter winds and
slight seas prevail in the rest of the gulf.

For the forecast, Hurricane Genevieve is near 13.9N 110.3W at 2
PM PDT, and is moving west-northwest at 12 kt. Maximum sustained
winds are 120 kt with gusts to 145 kt, and the minimum central 
pressure is 947 mb. Genevieve will move to 14.8N 111.7W Mon 
morning, 16.0N 113.4W Mon afternoon, 17.0N 114.9W Tue morning, 
17.6N 116.4W Tue afternoon, 18.3N 117.8W Wed morning, and 18.9N 
119.5W Wed afternoon. Genevieve will weaken as it moves NW to WNW 
Thu afternoon. Elsewhere, fresh to locally strong N winds 
expected in the Gulf of Tehuantepec through Monday. Winds may 
freshen off Baja California Norte by the end of the week.

High pressure north of the area is forcing fresh to locally
strong easterly trade winds and seas of 6-8 ft across the Gulf of
Papagayo region, extending westward to 93W. Elsewhere, moderate
or lighter winds and moderate seas are prevalent.

For the forecast, high pressure north of the area will force

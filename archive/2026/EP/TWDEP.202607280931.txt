Tropical Weather Discussion for the eastern Pacific Ocean from
03.4S to 30N, east of 120W including the Gulf of California, and
from the Equator to 30N, between 120W and 140W. The following
information is based on satellite imagery, weather observations,
radar, and meteorological analysis.

Based on 0600 UTC surface analysis and satellite imagery through
0900 UTC.
 
...SPECIAL FEATURES...

Hurricane Genevieve is centered near 17.5N 115.4W at 28/0900
UTC, moving northwest at 9 kt. Estimated minimum central
pressure is 943 mb. Maximum sustained wind speed is 120 kt with
gusts to 145 kt. Peak seas are near 40 ft or 12.5 m. Numerous
moderate convection is within 150 nm of the center. A west- 
northwestward to northwestward motion with some decrease in 
forward speed is expected during the next couple of days. 
Genevieve is a category 4 hurricane on the Saffir-Simpson 
Hurricane Wind Scale. Steady weakening is expected the next
several days. Swells generated by Genevieve area affecting the 
west coast of the Baja California Peninsula and portions of the 
southwestern coast of Mexico. These swells are likely to cause 
life- threatening surf and rip current conditions. Please consult
products from your local weather office. Please read the latest 
HIGH SEAS FORECAST issued by the National Hurricane Center at 
website - https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/text/MIAHSFEP2.shtml and the 
latest Genevieve NHC Forecast/Advisory and Public Advisory at 
www.hurricanes.gov for more details.

...TROPICAL WAVES...

A tropical wave is along 102W, south of 15N, moving westward at 
around 10 kt. Scattered moderate convection is observed from 03N
to 15N between 99W and 103W. 

...INTERTROPICAL CONVERGENCE ZONE/MONSOON TROUGH...

The monsoon trough extends from 1010 mb low pressure near 09N76W
to 08N93W to 10N107W. The ITCZ extends from 08N122W to beyond 
05N140W. Other than the convection mentioned with Genevieve and 
the tropical wave above, scattered moderate to isolated strong 
convection is noted from 01N to 09N between 77W and 92W and from 
00N to 08N between 128W and 140W.

...OFFSHORE WATERS WITHIN 250 NM OF MEXICO...

Please refer to the Special Features section for information on
Major Hurricane Genevieve. Please refer to the Tropical Waves 
and Monsoon Trough/ITCZ sections above for information about 
additional convection in the region.

Away from the impacts of Genevieve, moderate to locally fresh NW
winds associated with high pressure building in from the 
northwest are impacting waters N and W of Guadalupe Island. 
Outflow from convection over Sonora is bringing fresh SE winds to
the northern Gulf of California. Elsewhere, moderate or weaker 
winds prevail. Seas are slight in the Gulf of California and 
moderate in the remaining offshore waters.

For the forecast, Hurricane Genevieve is near 17.5N 115.4W at 2 
AM PDT, and is moving northwest at 9 kt. Maximum sustained winds 
are 120 kt with gusts to 145 kt, and the minimum central pressure
is 943 mb. Genevieve will move to 18.0N 116.5W this afternoon, 
18.6N 118.0W Wed morning, 19.1N 119.7W Wed afternoon, 19.8N 
121.8W Thu morning, and 20.5N 124.0W Thu afternoon. Genevieve
will weaken to a tropical storm near 21.3N 126.4W Fri morning.
Tropical Storm Genevieve will change little in intensity as it 
moves further west of Baja California Sur early Sat. Large seas 
associated with Genevieve will finally pull away from the 
offshore waters of Baja California Fri. Elsewhere, areas of 
fresh mainly westerly gap winds are likely through Wed night 
nearshore Baja California and also in the Gulf of California as 
high pressure builds southward to the west of the Baja California
Peninsula.

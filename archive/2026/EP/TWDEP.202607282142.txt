Tropical Weather Discussion for the eastern Pacific Ocean from
03.4S to 30N, east of 120W including the Gulf of California, and
from the Equator to 30N, between 120W and 140W. The following

Based on 1800 UTC surface analysis and satellite imagery through
2100 UTC.
 
...SPECIAL FEATURES...

Hurricane Genevieve is centered near 18.0N 116.9W at 28/2100
UTC, moving west-northwest at 8 kt. Estimated minimum central
pressure is 955 mb. Maximum sustained wind speed is 105 kt with
gusts to 130 kt. Peak seas are near 39 ft, or 12 m. Numerous 
moderate to strong convection is within 60 nm in the N
semicircle, 90 nm in the SE quadrant, and 75 nm in the SW
quadrant. Scattered to numerous moderate convection is 
elsewhere within 240 nm in the NE quadrant, 360 nm in the SE
quadrant, and 210 nm in the W semicircle. Genevieve is moving 
toward the west-northwest, and this general motion is expected 
during the next few days. Genevieve is a category 3 hurricane on 
the Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale. Steady weakening is 
forecast over the next several days. Swells generated by 
Genevieve are affecting portions of the southwestern coast of 
Mexico and the Baja California Peninsula. These swells are likely
to persist for a few more days, and could cause life-threatening
surf and rip current conditions. Please consult products from 
your local weather office. Please read the latest HIGH SEAS 
FORECAST issued by the National Hurricane Center at website - 
https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/text/MIAHSFEP2.shtml and the latest 
Genevieve NHC Forecast/Advisory and Public Advisory at 
www.hurricanes.gov for more details.
